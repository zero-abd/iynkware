# Fig. L10 — pH meter (Colorfigures prompt)

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of a **benchtop pH meter** on the pale green cutting mat with a flat
blue cast shadow: a dark grey meter 130 mm wide with a light front panel, a yellow-green LCD ("pH" and the reading to
0.01, 25.0 °C, CAL 3 or UNCAL), red ON and grey CAL and READ keys and a BNC socket; beside it a stand (heavy base,
230 mm steel post, a black swing arm) holding a 12 mm combination electrode (dark body, clear glass stem, round
bulb) over a 100 mL beaker of the liquid it is in. Its cable loops back to the meter's socket. In front, three white
buffer bottles with red, green and blue caps and labels (pH 4, pH 7, pH 10); behind, a wash bottle of distilled
water with a bent nozzle ("H₂O"). The blue storage cap sits on the bulb, or stands on the stand's base.

What it does:

- **Rinse the electrode**, **Calibrate** (pH 7, 4, 10 in turn; it ends in the pH 10 buffer), **Storage cap on/off**,
  **Power on/off**, **Electrode in ▾** (nothing, distilled water, dilute acid, dilute alkali, the three buffers).
  Pressing a buffer bottle dips the electrode in it, the wash bottle rinses it, the electrode dips or lifts it.
- The reading follows the liquid; uncalibrated it is up to a unit out; an unrinsed electrode drags the last liquid's
  pH into the next one.
- The tutor hears `liquid`, `calibrated`, `rinsed`, `capped`, `on`, `readingPH`, `truePH`, `carryOver`, `dryS` and
  `mistake`: `not-rinsed`, `not-calibrated`, `stored-dry` (out of its cap in the air for 10 s).

Look: Colorfigures ink and paper, flat bands, clear glass beaker, a glowing LCD.
