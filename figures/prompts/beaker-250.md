# Fig. L1 — 250 mL beaker (Colorfigures prompt)

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of a **250 mL low-form borosilicate beaker** standing alone on the pale
green cutting mat with a flat blue cast shadow: 70 mm across and 95 mm tall (ISO 3819), a thin clear glass wall drawn
as a faint tint so the liquid shows through it, a fire-polished rim with a small pouring spout at the back left, and
white enamel graduations on the front every 25 mL from 50 to 250 mL, numbered every 50, with "250 mL" and "Boro 3.3"
printed under them.

The liquid is a coloured lathe of the inside from the floor up to the level its volume gives (the level is computed
from the inside, so 150 mL sits on the 150 mark), lighter on its surface, with a thin darker meniscus ring.

What it does:

- **Liquid ▾**: water, copper sulfate solution (blue), potassium permanganate solution (purple), iodine solution
  (amber), universal indicator (green). **Volume ▾**: 0 to 300 mL in 50 mL steps.
- **Fill 50 mL** pours the level up, **Empty** drains it, **Hot plate on/off** heats it: the liquid warms at a rate
  that falls with its volume, bubbles rise from the floor near 100 °C and steam curls over the rim.
- The tutor hears `mistake`: `heating-empty`, `heating-too-full` (over 150 mL on the plate), `overfilled` (past 250).
- See-through fades the glass further and labels the spout, the graduations and the liquid level.

Look: Colorfigures ink and paper, the glass almost invisible except for its outline, rim and a white highlight
streak; the liquid in flat bands; heavy silhouette, finer inner lines.
