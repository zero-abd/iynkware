# Fig. L7 — Flame test kit (Colorfigures prompt)

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of a **school flame-test kit** on the pale green cutting mat: two rows
of four 50 mm clear watch glasses, each with a small heap of salt crystals on a white paper label in front of it
(LiCl, NaCl, KCl, CaCl₂ in front; CuCl₂, SrCl₂, BaCl₂ and a big "?" behind). The salts are white except the
blue-green copper(II) chloride, and the unknown looks like whichever salt it really is. Back right, a 50 mL beaker
(42 × 60 mm) of dilute hydrochloric acid on a "dil. HCl" label in red. Front right, a nichrome wire loop (a 4 mm
loop on 40 mm of wire, set in a 6 mm glass rod with a cork grip, 135 mm in all) lies from the mat across a small
wooden V-block stand, so the loop is in the air. The loop shows what it carries: bare silver wire when clean, a bead
of the salt's colour after a dip, with specks of a second colour when it carries two.

What it does:

- **Clean in acid** (the loop is dipped in the acid and burned clean), **Dip in the salt** (into the chosen dish),
  **Salt dish ▾** (the seven salts and the unknown), **The unknown is ▾** (which salt the "?" dish really holds).
  Pressing a dish dips the loop in it; pressing the acid beaker cleans it.
- The tutor hears `dish`, `unknown`, `clean`, `salt`, `carry`, `sample` (the metal the flame would show: sodium wins
  whenever it is on the loop), `mixed`, `flameColour` and `mistake`: `salt-before-clean` (the loop touched a salt
  before it was ever cleaned, so it carries sodium from fingers), `dirty-loop` (a second salt without cleaning).
- `sample` is what a host passes to the Bunsen burner's `sample` while the loop is held in the flame.

Look: Colorfigures ink and paper, flat bands, clear glass with an inked rim, flat white labels with black printing.
